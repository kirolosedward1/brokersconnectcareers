Most real estate job ads in Egypt say the same things: "attractive package", "generous commission", "motivating work environment". None of that means anything, and anyone who has worked this market knows the gap between two roles can be thousands of pounds a month.

The useful part of any listing comes down to four questions. If the ad does not answer them, ask before you apply.

## 1. Basic salary: a number, not an adjective

There is a real difference between a role paying EGP 6,000 basic with a lower commission and a role paying commission only. Both can be the right choice — for different people, at different points in their career.

If you are starting out, a basic salary buys you room to learn without worrying about the month's bills. If you already have a client base and a track record, the higher commission will usually pay you more.

Either way, know the number before you apply, not after the second interview.

## 2. Commission: the rate, and when it is paid

The percentage alone is not enough. Ask:

- What is it calculated on — the full unit price, or the company's net?
- When is it paid? On contract, or after a particular instalment clears?
- What happens if the client cancels after you have been paid?

That third one catches people out. Find out early.

## 3. Where the leads come from — the most important line in the ad

This question shapes your working day more than anything else.

**If the company supplies leads**, your job is conversion: you take enquiries generated by marketing and turn them into sales. Commission is usually lower, volume is higher, and sourcing clients is not your problem.

**If you generate your own**, commission is higher, but you are effectively running your own business under the company's licence. That needs an advertising budget, a network, or both.

**Hybrid** arrangements are common too. If it is hybrid, ask roughly what share of clients actually comes from the company.

There is no single right answer. The wrong outcome is discovering the answer in your first month.

## 4. The number of seats

An ad hiring one person is a different proposition from one hiring twenty-five.

A high seat count is not a bad sign in itself — companies open new teams, and that is ordinary in this market. But ask about training: how, for how long, and before you face clients or while you are already selling? And if the same large number reappears every couple of months, treat it as a signal about turnover.

## Before you close the tab

A listing that answers all four without being asked tells you something about the company: it is comfortable with its own terms and sees no reason to hide them.

If the answers are missing, ask. How a company responds to that question is half the interview.
