The first decision facing anyone entering Egyptian real estate is primary sales or resale. That choice shapes your working day far more than it shapes your LinkedIn title.

## Primary sales

You sell units directly from the developer — a project still being built or off plan.

**The day:** mostly clients arriving from marketing campaigns. You explain the project, payment plans and delivery dates, and compare developments.

**What decides success:** knowing the project in detail, and being able to read a client who cannot see the unit and has to picture it.

**Income:** usually a basic salary plus commission, paid in stages tied to the client's instalments. You can close today and receive part of your commission months later.

**The upside:** the company generally supplies leads, and training tends to be structured.

**The downside:** you are selling a product you do not control, and any developer delay lands on you in front of the client.

## Resale

You work with units that already exist, between two parties: a seller and a buyer.

**The day:** less structured, more movement. Viewings, price negotiation, and chasing paperwork and lawyers.

**What decides success:** your network. The units you hold, and the owners who trust you.

**Income:** a percentage of the transaction, sometimes from both sides. A single deal can be worth far more than a primary commission — and arrive far less regularly.

**The upside:** a higher income ceiling, and the client relationship belongs to you rather than the company.

**The downside:** you usually source your own clients. Without a network, the first months are hard.

## Which suits you

Neither track is better. One is better suited to your circumstances right now.

- **Starting out and need steady income?** Primary is safer: a basic salary, leads provided, and training.
- **Have a network in a particular district?** Resale makes better use of that asset.
- **Prefer structure and a clear target?** Primary.
- **Prefer independence and can carry the risk?** Resale.

Plenty of people spend a year or two in primary, build market knowledge and a network, then move to resale. That order makes sense.
